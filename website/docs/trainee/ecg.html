The 12-lead ECG room is a reading workstation. It puts one recording on calibrated paper and gives you the tools a reader reaches for: gain and paper speed, a filter chain, a magnifier, lead isolation, and calipers that span an interval and keep the reading.

This is a different thing from the rhythm waveform on the bedside monitor. The monitor on the Patient screen runs a live single-lead trace off the current heart rate and rhythm; this room holds a fixed twelve-lead recording that you measure and report on.

When the room appears

Look for 12-lead ECG with the subtitle interpretation in the bottom navigation bar. It appears when the platform runs the advanced release channel (the current channel is the pre-plugin build and carries no ECG room, see Install under "Which channel") and when the case you are in supplies a readable recording. A case carrying no recording leaves the button out of the bar.

What the case gives you

The room opens the recording the case author attached, built in the ECG case studio on the educator side. A recording arrives either as a signal (twelve leads of sample data, generated by the case or uploaded as encoded samples) or as an uploaded sheet (a PNG, JPEG, WebP or PDF of a printed tracing, opened in a simpler viewer where an image carries zoom, Rotate and Fit). A signal gets calibrated paper, filters and calipers, which is what the rest of this page describes.

Reading the paper

Above the sheet, a meta strip states the settings the tracing is drawn under: paper speed in mm/s, gain in mm/mV, sample rate in Hz, the filter band, and the view percentage once you zoom. The sheet itself is the conventional layout: three rows of four leads (I, aVR, V1, V4 / II, aVL, V2, V5 / III, aVF, V3, V6) with a full-width rhythm strip below, drawn from the recording's rhythm lead. Each row opens with a calibration pulse of 1 mV lasting 200 ms, so you can check the gain against the grid before measuring. The printed grid is 1 mm minor squares inside 5 mm major squares, toggled by Grid in the cart panel. Two view buttons sit at the top of the workspace: 12-lead for the full 3 × 4 sheet with its rhythm strip, and Lead focus for one lead as a single full-width strip, opening on lead II.

Zoom keeps millimetres honest

The zoom stepper moves through Fit, 150%, 250% and 400%, scaling the rendered sheet, so a caliper reading stays the same at any magnification. Lens adds a circular magnifier that follows your pointer at 2×, 3× or 4×, enlarging the grid along with the trace so the ruler magnifies too.

Cart settings

Click Cart to open the recording settings. Gain mm/mV offers 5 (half standard, for a tracing that clips), 10 (standard) and 20 (double standard, for low voltage). Speed mm/s offers 12.5 (half standard, more beats with less detail), 25 (standard) and 50 (double standard, for separating close deflections). A recording opens at 10 mm/mV and 25 mm/s.

Filters

Filter selects the chain the samples pass through before they are drawn. The chain is applied to the signal, so a wave measured through a filter gives the number that filter produces. A recording opens on Diagnostic.

  • Raw is unfiltered as acquired.
  • Diagnostic is 0.05–150 Hz, the band a 12-lead is reported from.
  • Monitor is 0.5–40 Hz, steadier, and able to create ST change.
  • 50 Hz and 60 Hz are the diagnostic band plus a mains notch at that frequency.

Monitor filtering changes what you may conclude

The 40 Hz roof shrinks R waves, and the 0.5 Hz high-pass steadies the baseline in a way that can manufacture ST depression. Use Monitor to settle a noisy trace, and report from Diagnostic.

The lead map

Click Lead map in the toolbar (the button carries the current lead) to open the selector beside the paper. Chest electrodes places V1 to V6 as dots on a patient figure at their landmarks, with the limb electrodes marked (RA, LA, LL, and RL as ground). Frontal plane places I, II, III, aVR, aVL and aVF on the hexaxial circle at their angles: I at 0°, II at +60°, III at +120°, aVF at +90°, aVL at -30°, aVR at -150°.

Selecting a lead focuses it and prints its origin and territory under the figures. The territories are:

  • Lateral wall: I, aVL, V5, V6
  • Inferior wall: II, III, aVF
  • Septum: V1, V2
  • Anterior wall: V3, V4
  • Cavity / outflow: aVR

Contiguity is the reason the grouping matters: ST change in two leads of one territory carries meaning that the same change scattered across territories lacks. Isolate dims every lead but the one under your pointer, which helps when a neighbouring trace crowds the one you are reading.

Taking a measurement

  1. Pick a caliper mode: Interval (time between two points), Amplitude (height between two points) or Rate (one RR span read as beats per minute).
  2. Click one point on the paper, then the second. The span appears with its reading printed above it.
  3. Drag either leg to walk it onto the exact onset; the reading follows.
  4. Read the detail line under the sheet: milliseconds, millivolts, paper millimetres, and the implied rate.

Every completed span is kept in the Measure tab with the lead it was taken on and the gain, speed and filter it was taken through. Clear calipers lifts the legs off the paper and leaves the recorded measurement in place. Two cart settings shape caliper behaviour: Caliper snap (Free, ½ mm or 1 mm, since a paper reading is a count of small squares) and March, which repeats the measured span along the sheet so you can walk out a rhythm or follow a P wave through a QRS.

The Measure tab

Each kept span becomes a row showing its value, its width in large squares, and the lead it came from. Name it from the row's label list: RR, PR, QRS, QT, P duration, ST level, Amplitude or Other. Naming is what makes a range apply: 400 ms is a normal QT and a fast RR. A named interval then carries a verdict of short, normal or long against the conventional adult range: RR 600 to 1000 ms, PR 120 to 200 ms, QRS 70 to 110 ms, QT 350 to 450 ms, P duration 60 to 120 ms.

When the record holds both a QT and an RR, the panel derives a corrected QT from the most recent of each and shows Bazett and Fridericia side by side with the heart rate it used. Discard removes a row.

The Systematic read tab

Fill the read as you work, in the order rate, rhythm, axis, intervals, morphology:

  1. Rate in bpm and Rhythm from the rhythm list.
  2. Frontal QRS axis, then PR, QRS and QT in ms.
  3. Findings, as checkboxes in four groups: Rate and rhythm, Conduction, ST segment and T wave, and Chambers and voltage.
  4. ECG statement, free text for what the tracing shows.

Describe the tracing here; what the findings mean for this patient is settled with the rest of the case.

The Notes tab

Write what you see as you see it. A note can be anchored to the lead you are focused on and can carry the last caliper reading, so the trail stays readable later. Notes hold up to 2000 characters, take Ctrl+Enter or Cmd+Enter to submit, and stay editable. A lead with notes attached shows a count badge on the lead map.

What is kept, and what you see

The room holds the persist capability, so your systematic read, your measurements and your notes are saved to the case as you work. The side rail's footer says as much: "Recorded to the case as you work. This ECG is one part of the picture."

The case document served to you is the learner projection: the author's rubric, which holds the expected findings, stays on the educator side and is stripped from what reaches this room. The room shows no score and asks for no diagnosis, and your read travels with the session into the debrief, where your instructor reviews it.
