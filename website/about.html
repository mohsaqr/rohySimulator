Designed by a physician and computer scientist.

Background

Mohammed Saqr is a Professor of Computer Science at the University of Eastern Finland, where he leads research in learning analytics. He studied, trained and practised medicine at the Faculty of Medicine, University of Alexandria, and specialised in Neurology at the university's teaching hospital. He taught clinical reasoning to medical students and residents, and has researched medicine, medical education, learning analytics and problem-based learning extensively.

Why Rohy looks the way it does

The clinical surfaces reflect what a clinician wants a trainee to encounter: a symptom-led interview with a patient who volunteers only what a patient would know, alarm-bounded vitals that keep deteriorating until they are treated, a family member who carries the history when the patient is unable to, a consultant page that takes time to answer, and reading rooms for DICOM, pathology and ECG that keep measurement separate from display.

The analytics surfaces are what a learning-analytics researcher expects to find: verb-coded events stamped with room and vitals, real-time clustering, sequence and process mining, transition networks, and affect and gaze recorded as aggregates with explicit uncertainty and privacy boundaries.

Research context

Rohy supports CRETIC — Optimizing Clinical Reasoning in Time-Critical Scenarios, a Research Council of Finland project led by Sonsoles López-Pernas at the School of Computing, University of Eastern Finland. The platform is built to serve that research agenda and works in any simulation context.
