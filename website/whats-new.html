Version 1.0.0 · April 2026

The baseline

The first public release shipped the core virtual-patient experience.

  • Text-based virtual patient chat with case-bound system prompts
  • A basic monitor
  • A single-room layout
  • Session persistence
  • An admin case editor
  • Multi-tenant authentication
Version 3.0.0-beta.9 · September 2026

The current line

The same core, inside an integrated clinical environment.

  • Voice, 3D avatars, lipsync and a 3D bedside room
  • Patient, nurse, consultant, family and discussant agents
  • Core rooms for the patient, examination, laboratory, radiology and debrief, plus Bedside, 12-lead ECG, Pathology and PACS plugin rooms
  • Courses, lessons, surveys, rosters and assigned access
  • Oyon affect, gaze, head pose, posture and rPPG capture
  • Transition-network analytics with clustering
Simulation platform

The encounter became multimodal, multi-agent and multi-room.

Rohy moved from a patient chat surface to a structured clinical environment with voice, avatars, rooms, investigations, reading instruments, treatments and debrief.

Voice, avatars and lipsync

Students interact by text or voice with animated 3D patient avatars.

  • Google, OpenAI, Kokoro and Piper text-to-speech, with local Piper voice packs per language.
  • Viseme-driven lipsync over 28 bundled GLB heads and voice-mode subtitles.
  • Case-owned voice identity with per-persona rate and pitch.
  • Browser speech-to-text with continuous mode.

Multi-agent care team

The learner addresses a team.

  • Patient, nurse, consultant, family and debrief discussant personas.
  • Page and call flows with server-anchored arrival timing.
  • Separate language-model routing per persona.
  • Knowledge scope controlled per agent and per case.

A room for each task

The single room became a clinical workspace.

  • Patient, Examination, Laboratory, Radiology and Consultant as core rooms; Bedside, 12-lead ECG, Pathology and PACS as plugin rooms.
  • Room activity badges for ready results.
  • Room-stamped learning events.
  • Refresh-safe navigation state. Every room →

Reading instruments

Clinical media are read and measured.

  • A DICOM reading room with multi-series studies, dual viewports, and window, level, gamma and transfer-function controls.
  • A whole-slide pathology viewer with a magnification ladder, scale bar and measuring annotations.
  • A 12-lead ECG workstation with calibrated paper, filters, calipers and a lead map.
  • Display adjustments labelled as display only in all three.

Investigations

Laboratory and imaging behave as clinical tasks.

  • Over 200 laboratory tests in more than 30 groups.
  • 74 radiology and diagnostic studies.
  • Sex-specific reference ranges.
  • A one-to-five-minute turnaround band, worklists, and viewed states.

Structured examination

The exam surface is specific and teachable.

  • 67 named regions on anterior, posterior and lateral maps.
  • Inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation, plus special tests.
  • Site-specific auscultation audio.
  • An examination log that the debrief receives.

Live physiology and treatment

The monitor carries the case's temporal spine.

  • Real-time ECG synthesis with rhythms and modifiers, and multi-parameter vitals.
  • A treatment engine with 33 default treatments and onset, peak and duration.
  • Alarm routing through one central notification dispatcher.
  • Case snapshots frozen at session start.

Debrief

The post-case phase has its own room and persona.

  • A separate Socratic discussant with its own voice, avatar and model.
  • The learner's encounter record supplied to the discussant since 2.9.97.
  • Transcript, notes and case summary alongside the conversation.
  • Structured feedback captured for cohort export.

Bedside

Rohy 3 adds a bedside room.

  • The patient in bed with the live monitor, a camera wheel and an examination wheel.
  • One conversation shared with the Patient room.
  • Findings that open the same chart and persist like the 2D room's.
  • Shipped as a plugin through the new plugin capability surface.
Teaching operations

Courses, users, enrolment and assignment are core product areas.

The platform supports teaching administration: rosters, registration codes, assignment, bulk operations, course reports, lessons, surveys and controlled learner access.

Courses and cohorts

Teachers manage classes.

  • Teacher-owned courses and classes.
  • Join and registration codes.
  • Co-teachers and course settings.
  • Per-course case assignment.

Lessons and surveys

Cases sit inside authored teaching material.

  • Rich lessons authored by educators.
  • Surveys collected per course.
  • A student classroom experience on the same platform.

Roster and completion

Course reporting is built around enrolled people.

  • Roster views and a completion grid.
  • A live feed.
  • Course export.

Registration governance

Enrolment is controlled by policy.

  • Open, closed, invite-only and administrator-approval modes.
  • Email-domain rules and revocable invitation links and codes.
  • Course auto-enrolment and an approval queue that stores only password hashes.
  • Fresh installations default to closed registration.

Enterprise user operations

User administration scales to larger deployments.

  • Bulk operations and a bulk import workflow.
  • Enrolment and assignment flows aligned across users, courses and cases.
  • Target-rank ceilings so administrators manage only accounts below their own rank.

Controlled student access

Students see the cases assigned to them.

  • The default course and default case remain available.
  • Assigned course cases are visible to enrolled students.
  • Unassigned cases are blocked from direct API access and from session launch.

Six languages

Language is a property of the case.

  • English, German, Spanish, Italian, Finnish and Swedish across the learner and instructor interface.
  • Language-aware cases, voices and model directives.
  • Immutable case languages and codes.

Settings Control Center and onboarding

Administration starts from a command deck.

  • A card-based settings landing page with Courses as a top-level area.
  • Oyon and Logs grouped under Analytics.
  • A first-run setup checklist for administrators and role-appropriate welcome flows for learners and teachers.

Documentation and help

Operators and educators have a support surface.

  • A VitePress documentation site.
  • A role-filtered in-app Help & Support drawer.
  • Release notes and a diagnostics bundle.
  • Trainee, educator, admin, operator, integrator and security guides.
Research and analytics

Rohy produces analysable multimodal learning traces.

The analytics layer connects user activity, course context, rooms, cases, vitals, Oyon signals, logs and learning sequences.

Oyon capture and analytics

Browser-side affect and gaze capture is integrated into the simulation.

  • MediaPipe and ONNX Runtime Web inference in the tab.
  • Oyon 3.3.2 with eight-emotion classification, valence and arousal, gaze, head pose, action units, posture and rPPG heart rate.
  • Aggregated ten-second windows and normalised gaze targets.
  • Gaze maps by simulator room.

Patient eye contact and affect routing

The avatar reacts to the learner.

  • Patient and discussant track the learner's face position.
  • The avatar glances at the monitor when an alarm fires.
  • Optional, consent-bound affect routing tells the patient how the learner appears, restricted to local providers by default.

Learning-event log

Simulation activity is encoded for analysis.

  • Over 130 canonical verbs.
  • Room-stamped and vitals-enriched events with affect columns.
  • A session activity feed.
  • A team-communications log per session.

Course analytics

Teachers get course and student views.

  • Course filters in analytics and logs.
  • Student activity frequencies.
  • Case and session drill-downs.

Sequence and network analysis

The analytics stack supports research patterns directly.

  • Transition-network analytics with clustering and a silhouette score.
  • State distributions and temporal proportion plots per cluster.
  • Timeline and hourly views.
  • A shared, versioned TNA bundle.

Data safety and visibility

Research data exposure is controlled.

  • Frozen-at-write Oyon visibility flags.
  • Tenant and ownership gates on sessions, orders, results and staff access.
  • Centralised response redaction.
  • Role-aware access checks and rate limits.
Release arc

The change by product generation.

The progression from the first public release to the current 3.0 beta. Full entries are in the repository changelog and on the releases page.

v1.0.0
April 2026

Initial public release

Virtual-patient chat, a basic monitor, a single-room workflow, session persistence, a case editor and multi-tenant authentication.

v2.0.0
14 May 2026

Simulation expansion

Voice, avatars, the multi-agent care team, multi-room navigation, Oyon emotion capture, richer investigations, the real monitor, event logging, case snapshots, the landing site and the operator update tool.

Prompt stability, notification consolidation, tenant middleware, text-to-speech routing fixes, deployment fixes and the May 2026 security hardening.

v2.1.x
14 May 2026

Persona-level model routing

Patient, discussant and agents route through their own configured language-model settings. The version badge, prompt hardening and default turnaround cleanup landed in this line.

v2.3.x
16 May 2026 onward

Teacher cohorts and documentation

Teacher-owned classes, join codes, roster, completion grid, cohort analytics, per-course assignment and co-teachers.

The documentation site, in-app Help & Support, a bug-fix pass, CI hardening and install fixes.

v2.4.x – v2.5.x
May – July 2026

Oyon v2 analytics, gaze, and enterprise courses

Oyon v2 analytics, gaze capture, room-specific gaze maps, transition heatmaps, shared TNA graphs and normalised gaze targets. Capture persistence through session start and debrief.

Patient eye contact, the monitor and header redesign, the Settings Control Center, enterprise user and enrolment work, course analytics, roster and report improvements, and class-based case access.

v2.7.0
12 July 2026

The affect-aware patient

When an administrator enables affect routing, the AI patient is told each turn how the learner currently appears from consent-gated Oyon capture and reacts in character. Off by default, restricted to local providers unless widened, and gated on capture consent.

v2.8.0
29 July 2026

Courses, lessons, surveys and six languages

The first published release of the 2.6 and 2.7 development line. Courses, lessons and surveys; German, Spanish, Italian, Finnish and Swedish alongside English; Voice 2.0 with case-owned voice identity; first-run onboarding; registration governance; consent-bound affect routing.

Closed session and order authorisation gaps, enforced tenant and case binding, and target-rank ceilings for administrators. The published multi-arch Docker image is tagged from this release.

v2.9.x
29 July – 1 September 2026

Oyon 3, reading instruments, and the encounter-aware debrief

Oyon upgraded to the immutable 3.3.2 release with sensing, typing, voice, interaction, discourse and analytics modules and a versioned TNA bundle. Atomic, checksummed model downloads with a fail-fast verifier.

The DICOM reading room, the whole-slide pathology viewer and the 12-lead ECG workstation shipped as plugins in this line, alongside the Pathology room. From 2.9.97 the debrief discussant receives the learner's encounter record.

v3.0.0-beta.9
2 September 2026

Bedside

A 3D bedside room as the second room in the navigator, rendered from a sibling package as a plugin. The patient lies in bed with the live monitor, the camera wheel and the examination wheel; findings open the same chart and persist like the 2D room's; the chart and IV pole open Records and Treatments. The major bump marks the new sibling dependency and the plugin capability surface. Existing APIs and data shapes are unchanged.

Bottom line

One platform surface.

The important shift is the integration. Cases, courses, users, assigned access, agents, rooms, vitals, voice, reading instruments, Oyon, logs, analytics, exports and documentation work as one platform surface.
